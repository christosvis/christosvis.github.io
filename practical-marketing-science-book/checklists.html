Practical Marketing Science

C All checklists in one place

Every chapter ends with a checklist. They are collected here for use as a pre-flight list before an analysis, a test or a readout. Each heading links back to the chapter that explains the items.

What marketing science is for

Chapter 1.

  • What decision will this analysis inform, and who will make it?
  • What would we do if the answer came out high? If it came out low? If both are the same, stop.
  • Is the question about attributed, incremental or marginal performance? Is the method able to answer that question?
  • Which evidence layer does the method belong to, and does a higher layer already disagree with it?
  • What precision is needed for the decision, and can the planned design deliver it?
  • Is there a tracking change, naming change or promotion in the window that would break a comparison?

Data and signal foundations

Chapter 2.

  • Is the outcome taken from the system of record, not from tracking?
  • Are tracked and backend outcomes stored side by side, with their ratio monitored?
  • Is there a change log, and does any entry overlap the analysis window?
  • Is unmapped spend below an agreed threshold for every week in the window?
  • Have currency, time zone and duplicate checks been run?
  • Does every channel you want to measure have enough spend variation to learn from?
  • Are all channels included, including offline ones, so that omitted spend does not bias the rest?

Causal thinking for marketers

Chapter 3.

  • Have you written down the counterfactual: what exactly would have happened without the treatment?
  • Have you drawn the causal graph, including how the budget was set?
  • Which variables are confounders (adjust) and which are mediators or colliders (do not adjust)?
  • Does the analysis filter on anything that happens after treatment?
  • Where on the identification ladder does the method sit, and is the decision large enough to justify climbing higher?
  • Does the readout state the design, the uncertainty and the confounders that remain?

Bayesian reasoning in practice

Chapter 4.

  • Are priors set and documented before fitting, with a source for each informative prior?
  • Has a prior predictive check confirmed that the priors imply plausible outcomes?
  • Has a sensitivity analysis shown whether a different reasonable prior changes the decision?
  • When combining a model and an experiment, do they measure the same quantity (spend level, period, market, outcome)?
  • Was the decision rule (thresholds on probability and expected loss) fixed before looking at results?
  • Does the readout report probability of loss and expected loss, not just a point estimate?

A/B tests that hold up

Chapter 5.

  • Is there a written plan with hypothesis, unit, primary metric and window, MDE, sample size, duration and decision rule, dated before launch?
  • Does the MDE correspond to an effect size worth acting on? If not, redesign or do not run the test.
  • Does the test run in whole weeks and long enough for the outcome window?
  • Was the sample ratio checked, and were A/A tests run on this setup?
  • Is the decision metric read only at the planned end, or under a pre-planned sequential design?
  • How many comparisons does the readout make, and are they corrected or labeled as exploratory?
  • Is a null result reported with the effect size that could have been detected?

Geo experiments

Chapter 6.

  • Is the geo unit the smallest one at which media and outcomes can both be separated, with spillover considered?
  • Was assignment randomized (ideally stratified), and is the method fixed in advance?
  • Does a pre-test power analysis on historical data give an MDE worth testing for?
  • Is the treatment type (go-dark, heavy-up, scale-back) matched to the decision (average versus marginal return)?
  • Is delivery verified by region, so that the treatment actually happened as designed?
  • Is the outcome taken from the system of record, with a cooldown window for lasting effects?
  • Is the result reported as an iROAS range from placebo-based or model-based intervals, with the MDE?

Quasi-experiments: DiD, CausalImpact and synthetic control

Chapter 7.

  • Is there a plausible comparison that was not affected by the intervention?
  • For DiD: are event-study pre-period coefficients flat, and are standard errors clustered at the unit level?
  • For staggered rollouts: is a staggered-adoption estimator used instead of plain two-way fixed effects?
  • For CausalImpact: are the controls unaffected, chosen on pre-period grounds only, and fixed before looking?
  • Do placebo-in-time (and, where possible, placebo-in-space) tests come out null?
  • Does the change log show anything else hitting the treated unit in the window?
  • Does the readout name the design as quasi-experimental and list the threats that remain?

Reading platform lift studies

Chapter 8.

  • Is the outcome the platform’s tracked conversion or your system-of-record conversion?
  • Is the result intent-to-treat or per reached user, and does any comparison mix the two?
  • How many conversions were in the holdout, and what MDE did the design have?
  • Which confidence or credibility level is reported?
  • Did budget, bidding or creative change during the study?
  • Is the incrementality factor computed with its interval and stored with the conditions it was measured under (platform, campaign type, market, attribution setting, date)?
  • If the result is applied to another market, campaign or spend level, is that stated as an assumption?

Marketing mix modeling end to end

Chapter 9.

  • Is the outcome from the system of record, and are all major non-media drivers in the base?
  • Does every modeled channel have meaningful spend variation (check the coefficient of variation), and are channels without it grouped or handled with priors?
  • Is there a channel whose spend responds to demand (search, retargeting)? Treat its estimate as an upper bound until tested.
  • Are adstock and saturation choices justified per channel type?
  • Is the ratio of channels to observations reasonable, or is geo-level data available?
  • Is anyone treating R² or MAPE as validation?

Bayesian MMM in practice

Chapter 10.

  • Is every prior written down with a reason, and does the prior predictive check imply plausible ROIs and media shares?
  • Were divergences, R-hat and effective sample sizes checked before reading results?
  • Does the posterior predictive check cover the data at roughly the nominal rate?
  • For each channel, how does the posterior compare with the prior? Which channels are data-driven and which are prior-driven?
  • Which channels’ spend responds to demand? Are their estimates flagged as likely upward-biased until tested?

Validating and calibrating MMM

Chapter 11.

  • Has the model been refit on several windows, and are unstable channels flagged as unidentified?
  • Is every channel’s estimate compared with an experiment where one exists?
  • For channels whose spend responds to demand, is there a demand proxy that is a confounder (category level) rather than a mediator (brand level)?
  • Are experiments used for calibration matched to the model’s outcome, market and spend level, with uncertainty inflated where they are not?
  • Has parameter recovery been checked on data simulated to resemble the real situation?
  • Is there an experiment plan for channels that remain unidentified?

Response curves and budget optimization

Chapter 12.

  • Is the decision based on marginal ROI at current spend, not average ROI?
  • Is the break-even threshold set on profit (margin-adjusted), not revenue?
  • Are the curves from a calibrated model, and are channels with confounded or prior-dominated estimates flagged?
  • Are optimizer bounds kept within the historically observed spend range?
  • Is the expected gain reported with its uncertainty and the probability of a loss?
  • Is the recommendation implemented as a measurable step (heavy-up or scale-back test) rather than all at once?

Attribution and its limits

Chapter 13.

  • Is the attribution output being used to compare channels or set budgets? If so, stop and look for incrementality evidence.
  • Are attributed conversions labeled as attributed in every report, never as incremental?
  • For each channel, is its targeting correlated with existing intent (retargeting, CRM, branded search)? Expect over-credit.
  • Where an incrementality factor exists, is it applied, with its date and conditions?
  • How much of the journey is invisible because of consent, devices or platforms, and which channels does that hurt?

Triangulation: deciding under contradictory evidence

Chapter 14.

  • Does each source have a stated bias allowance as well as its reported uncertainty?
  • Is attribution kept out of incrementality combinations, or given a large allowance?
  • Is the decision made on marginal returns, with probability and size of loss reported?
  • Is the reversibility of the decision considered alongside expected value?
  • Is the testing roadmap prioritized by money at stake and the chance of flipping a decision?
  • Is there a channel scorecard with evidence type, age and confidence, kept up to date?

Cohorts, retention, LTV and payback

Chapter 15.

  • Are retention and value analyzed by cohort (acquisition month and channel), not as blended averages?
  • Does the retention model allow churn to fall as cohorts age, rather than assuming a constant rate?
  • Is lifetime value built from margin, over a stated horizon, with discounting where the horizon is long?
  • Is the uncertainty of projected LTV reported, and how much of it is projection beyond observed months?
  • Are channel CAC targets set on value, and on incremental rather than attributed acquisition cost?
  • For experiments that change customer mix, is value read at a long enough horizon?

Churn and survival analysis

Chapter 16.

  • Are churn and lifetime estimated with methods that handle censoring (Kaplan-Meier, Cox, parametric survival), not averages over churned customers?
  • Is the churn definition explicit and stable, with voluntary and involuntary churn separated?
  • Are hazard ratios presented as associations unless the feature was randomized?
  • Are retention offers targeted by uplift from a randomized test, rather than by predicted risk?
  • Is the effect of retention interventions read at a horizon long enough to distinguish prevention from delay?

Segmentation

Chapter 17.

  • Does each segment lead to a different, feasible action, and can a customer’s segment be identified when the action happens?
  • Has a simple managerial segmentation (such as RFM) been tried as a baseline?
  • Were features transformed and scaled deliberately, with the choice of what counts as “different” written down?
  • Is the number of segments justified by actionability, with stability checked rather than silhouette alone?
  • Are categorical features handled with an appropriate distance (Gower) rather than raw one-hot encoding?
  • Are segments profiled on outcomes that were not clustering inputs, and are segment-specific treatments tested?

Brand measurement

Chapter 18.

  • Is brand health tracked with a fixed set of metrics and a fixed competitor set, by market, over time?
  • Is share of search used rather than raw branded search volume, and are changes rather than levels correlated?
  • Is there a change log for events that move branded search (paid brand bidding, PR, incidents)?
  • Are penetration and new-customer metrics tracked alongside loyalty metrics?
  • Do performance experiments also record halo outcomes (branded search, direct, organic)?
  • Are brand effects judged on windows long enough for them to appear?

Pricing research and elasticities

Chapter 19.

  • Is the method matched to the decision: surveys for ranges and feature values, tests for final price changes?
  • Are stated-intent results treated as relative signals, calibrated where possible against real conversion?
  • Has the break-even volume change been calculated for the proposed price change at the actual margin?
  • Are historical elasticities treated as confounded context rather than estimates?
  • Does any price test respect fairness and regulatory constraints, and is it read on margin per customer over a long enough horizon, including mix effects?

Forecasting and anomaly detection

Chapter 20.

  • Is every forecast compared with a seasonal naive baseline in a rolling-origin backtest at the relevant horizon?
  • Do prediction intervals achieve close to their nominal coverage in the backtest?
  • Are holidays, promotions and known events in the model, so that expected dips are not treated as surprises?
  • Do monitoring alerts compare each day with a model expectation, scaled by the model’s normal error, rather than with yesterday?
  • Are persistent level shifts checked with change-point or cumulative methods, not only single-day thresholds?
  • Is each alert routed to an owner, with the change log checked before escalating?

Paid search

Chapter 21.

  • Is brand search incrementality measured with a randomized design (regional switchback or geo holdout) on total brand traffic?
  • Are brand and non-brand reported and targeted separately?
  • Are bids and budgets set on marginal CPA, not average CPA?
  • Do automated campaigns receive system-of-record conversions and values, and exclude existing customers?
  • Is the search channel’s MMM estimate treated as likely upward-biased until calibrated (Chapter 11)?

Paid social and creative fatigue

Chapter 22.

  • Is paid social incrementality measured with lift studies on system-of-record outcomes, and checked with geo tests for large budgets?
  • Are creative tests run with randomized audiences, on acquisition metrics, with a fixed sample size?
  • Is fatigue measured against frequency rather than calendar days, with a refresh threshold derived from your own data?
  • Do click-based fatigue signals agree with conversion-based ones?
  • Are frequency caps tested rather than set by convention?

SEO measurement

Chapter 23.

  • Can the change be tested by randomizing comparable pages into changed and control groups?
  • Is randomization stratified by pre-period traffic, and has a placebo analysis shown the design can detect a realistic effect?
  • Does the readout window allow for crawling and re-ranking?
  • Are site-wide changes analyzed with a site-level quasi-experiment instead of a page split?
  • Are search engine updates and site releases in the change log?

CRM and email holdouts

Chapter 24.

  • Does every CRM program have a randomized holdout, sized from the effect that matters?
  • Is there a persistent global holdout measuring the cumulative effect of all messaging?
  • Are results read on the outcome (activation, transactions, revenue), not on opens or post-click conversions?
  • Are unsubscribes, complaints and long-run engagement tracked as guardrails?
  • Are message A/B tests run with a no-message holdout still in place?

Readouts that change decisions

Chapter 25.

  • Does the readout open with the verdict and the confidence in it?
  • Is the design named in the first paragraph, with observational results labeled as such?
  • Does every estimate have an interval, a sample size, a time window and a source?
  • Are null results reported with the effect size that could have been detected?
  • Are the number of comparisons and any interim looks disclosed?
  • Do charts show intervals and decision thresholds?
  • Does the recommendation have an owner, a date and a statement of what would change it?

The analyst’s toolkit

Chapter 26.

  • Is there a written plan, dated before the data, for every confirmatory analysis?
  • Was the design checked by simulation, including its power and false positive rate?
  • Is the analysis reproducible: code in version control, seeds fixed, package versions and data snapshot recorded?
  • Are AI-generated queries and analyses reviewed against the metric dictionary and checked for silent choices?
  • Is there a shared record of past experiments and results that new work can draw on?
