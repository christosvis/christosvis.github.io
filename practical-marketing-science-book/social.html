Chapter 22 Paid social and creative fatigue

Whether paid social prospecting is incremental, how to test creatives fairly, and when a creative has worn out and needs replacing.

22.1 What makes paid social different

Paid social platforms sell attention in feeds, and their delivery algorithms decide who sees which ad. Three consequences shape how it should be measured.

  • The platform chooses the audience. Optimization toward conversions sends ads to people the algorithm predicts will convert, many of whom would have converted anyway. Attributed conversions overstate incrementality, often by a large factor (Chapters 1 and 8).
  • Creative is the main lever. With targeting increasingly automated, the creative does most of the work of reaching the right people and persuading them. Creative testing and refresh are where much of the performance comes from.
  • Effects extend beyond the click. Video and upper-funnel formats drive branded search, direct visits and later conversions that the platform cannot see. Lift studies on platform outcomes and geo tests on backend outcomes (Chapter 6) see different parts of this.

For incrementality, use the platform’s conversion lift studies with system-of-record conversions where possible, and geo tests for the biggest budget decisions. The rest of this chapter is about creative.

22.2 Testing creatives fairly

Putting several creatives in one ad set and comparing their results is not a test. The delivery algorithm shifts spend toward whichever creative looks better in its first impressions, gives the others little delivery, and shows them to different audiences. The comparison is confounded by who saw what and when, and early noise becomes a self-fulfilling verdict.

A fair creative test needs:

  • Randomized audiences: the platform’s split-test or A/B feature, which assigns users to cells and holds delivery comparable.
  • A metric that matters: cost per acquired customer (or better, per incremental customer), not click-through rate. Creatives that maximize clicks are often not the ones that convert.
  • Enough volume and a fixed duration, set from the expected difference and the baseline rate (Chapter 5).

22.3 Creative fatigue

As the same people see the same creative again and again, response falls. That decline is fatigue, and its rate determines how often new creative is needed. It is best measured against frequency (average impressions per person reached), not calendar time, because a creative with a big budget wears out faster than the same creative with a small one.

22.3.1 Worked example: estimating how fast response decays

Twelve creatives run for 60 days each, launched at different times, with different budgets and different baseline appeal. Each creative’s click-through rate falls exponentially with its average frequency. We estimate the decay rate across all creatives at once with a Poisson regression of clicks on frequency, with a separate baseline for each creative and impressions as exposure.

import numpy as np, pandas as pd, warnings
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

rng = np.random.default_rng(22)
K_TRUE = 0.18                                     # CTR falls by this rate per unit of average frequency
rows = []
for c in range(12):
    base_ctr = rng.uniform(0.006, 0.015)
    daily_imps = rng.uniform(20_000, 80_000)
    audience = rng.uniform(150_000, 400_000)
    cum = 0.0
    for d in range(60):
        imps = rng.poisson(daily_imps)
        cum += imps
        reach = audience * (1 - np.exp(-cum / audience))
        freq = cum / reach                         # average frequency so far
        clicks = rng.binomial(imps, base_ctr * np.exp(-K_TRUE * (freq - 1)))
        rows.append({"creative": c, "day": d, "impressions": imps, "clicks": clicks, "frequency": freq})
ads = pd.DataFrame(rows)

fit = smf.glm("clicks ~ C(creative) + frequency", ads, family=sm.families.Poisson(),
              offset=np.log(ads["impressions"])).fit()
k = -fit.params["frequency"]
lo, hi = -fit.conf_int().loc["frequency"][::-1]
print(f"Estimated decay per unit of frequency: {k:.3f} (95% CI {lo:.3f} to {hi:.3f}); true {K_TRUE}")
## Estimated decay per unit of frequency: 0.179 (95% CI 0.177 to 0.181); true 0.18
print(f"CTR halves after about {np.log(2) / k:.1f} additional impressions per person")
## CTR halves after about 3.9 additional impressions per person
print(f"CTR falls to 60% of launch level at an average frequency of about {1 + np.log(1 / 0.6) / k:.1f}")
## CTR falls to 60% of launch level at an average frequency of about 3.9
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
ads["rel_ctr"] = ads["clicks"] / ads["impressions"] / np.exp(
    fit.params["Intercept"] + fit.params.filter(like="C(creative)").reindex(
        [f"C(creative)[T.{c}]" for c in ads["creative"]]).fillna(0).to_numpy() - k)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 2.7))
ax.scatter(ads["frequency"], ads["rel_ctr"], s=6, alpha=0.4, color="#6B6B6B")
f = np.linspace(1, ads["frequency"].max(), 100)
ax.plot(f, np.exp(-k * (f - 1)), lw=2.2, color="#1F4E79", label="Estimated decay")
ax.axhline(0.6, ls=":", color="#C8102E", label="Refresh threshold (60%)")
ax.set_xlabel("Average frequency")
ax.set_ylabel("CTR relative to launch")
ax.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Figure 22.1: Daily CTR relative to each creative’s launch level, against average frequency, with the estimated decay curve.

The pooled model recovers the true decay rate and turns it into a rule that planners can use: refresh a creative when its audience’s average frequency approaches the threshold. Because the rule is in terms of frequency, it automatically schedules earlier refreshes for creatives with larger budgets or smaller audiences.

Three cautions apply. Click-through is a convenient proxy, but fatigue in conversion rate or cost per acquisition is what matters, and it is noisier; check that the two move together before relying on clicks. Fatigue rates differ by format, audience and market, so estimate them on your own data and refresh the estimate. And frequency is not only a fatigue problem: some exposure is needed before response appears at all, so the right frequency is a balance, best found with tests of different frequency caps.

Declaring a winning creative from an ad set where the algorithm chose the delivery. Use the platform’s split-testing feature, or a holdout design, and judge on acquisition cost, not click-through.

22.4 Checklist

  • Is paid social incrementality measured with lift studies on system-of-record outcomes, and checked with geo tests for large budgets?
  • Are creative tests run with randomized audiences, on acquisition metrics, with a fixed sample size?
  • Is fatigue measured against frequency rather than calendar days, with a refresh threshold derived from your own data?
  • Do click-based fatigue signals agree with conversion-based ones?
  • Are frequency caps tested rather than set by convention?
