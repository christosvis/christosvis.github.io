Chapter 21 Paid search

How much of paid search is incremental, especially on brand terms, how to set bids and budgets on marginal rather than average cost, and which tests settle the question.

21.1 Why search needs special care

Paid search captures demand rather than creating most of it. People who search for your brand were already looking for you; people who search for the category are already in the market. That makes search look excellent in attribution (Chapter 13) and dangerous in an MMM (Chapter 9), where its spend moves with demand. The two questions that matter are how much of it is incremental, and what the next euro buys.

21.2 Brand terms: how much would you get anyway?

When you stop bidding on your own brand name, many of the people who would have clicked the ad click the organic result instead. The incremental value of brand search is the share of traffic that does not come back, plus whatever protection the ads give against competitors bidding on your name. Both vary by market and by how aggressively competitors bid, so it has to be measured.

A clean design is a switchback test across regions: randomly assign each region-week to brand ads on or off, so that every region spends some weeks in each state, and compare total brand-search visits (paid plus organic).

21.2.1 Worked example

Twenty regions, twelve weeks, brand ads on or off at random each region-week. Without ads, 80% of brand searches end in an organic click. With ads on, the ad takes half of those clicks and adds a small number of visits that would not have happened otherwise (8% of searches). So roughly five in six paid brand clicks would have arrived organically anyway.

import numpy as np, pandas as pd, warnings
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

rng = np.random.default_rng(21)
R, W = 20, 12
rows = []
size = rng.lognormal(np.log(2000), 0.5, R)
week_effect = 1 + 0.08 * np.sin(np.arange(W) / 2)
for r in range(R):
    for w in range(W):
        on = int(rng.random() < 0.5)
        searches = rng.poisson(size[r] * week_effect[w])
        organic_without_ads = rng.binomial(searches, 0.80)
        incremental = rng.binomial(searches, 0.08) if on else 0          # visits only the ad produces
        captured = rng.binomial(organic_without_ads, 0.5) if on else 0   # would-be organic clicks taken by the ad
        rows.append({"region": r, "week": w, "ads_on": on,
                     "paid_clicks": captured + incremental,
                     "visits": organic_without_ads + incremental})
df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
df["log_visits"] = np.log(df["visits"])

fit = smf.ols("log_visits ~ ads_on + C(region) + C(week)", df).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["region"]})
lift = np.exp(fit.params["ads_on"]) - 1
lo, hi = np.exp(fit.conf_int().loc["ads_on"]) - 1
on = df[df.ads_on == 1]
incr_visits = on["visits"].sum() * lift / (1 + lift)
print(f"Brand-search visits with ads on vs off: {lift:+.1%} (95% CI {lo:+.1%} to {hi:+.1%})")
## Brand-search visits with ads on vs off: +9.8% (95% CI +9.0% to +10.6%)
print(f"Paid brand clicks in 'on' weeks: {on['paid_clicks'].sum():,.0f}; implied incremental visits: {incr_visits:,.0f} "
      f"({incr_visits / on['paid_clicks'].sum():.0%} of paid clicks)")
## Paid brand clicks in 'on' weeks: 144,237; implied incremental visits: 23,557 (16% of paid clicks)

Total brand-search visits are only about 10% higher with ads on, and only about one paid brand click in six is incremental. If brand CPC is 0.30 EUR, the cost per incremental visit is around six times that. Whether brand bidding pays depends on that incremental cost against the value of a visit, and on what competitors do in your absence. Those are empirical questions, and the answer differs between markets: where competitors bid aggressively on your name, the incremental share is much higher.

Turning brand search off nationally for two weeks and comparing with the two weeks before. Seasonality, other campaigns and competitor moves all change in the same window. Randomize across regions and weeks, and measure total brand traffic, not paid clicks.

21.3 Non-brand terms: marginal, not average

For non-brand terms, the issue is less incrementality (most non-brand clicks would not have reached you otherwise) than diminishing returns. Raising bids buys more clicks at higher prices; each extra conversion costs more than the last. Search platforms’ bid simulators, or your own spend tests, trace a curve of conversions against spend. A power curve, conversions \(= a \cdot \text{spend}^b\) with \(b < 1\), usually fits well.

a, b = 0.8, 0.62                       # fitted from bid-simulator points, for example
spend = np.array([5_000, 10_000, 20_000, 30_000, 40_000, 60_000])
conv = a * spend ** b
avg_cpa = spend / conv
marg_cpa = 1 / (a * b * spend ** (b - 1))           # derivative of spend w.r.t. conversions
print(pd.DataFrame({"monthly spend": spend, "conversions": conv.round(0),
                    "average CPA": avg_cpa.round(0), "marginal CPA": marg_cpa.round(0)}).to_string(index=False))
##  monthly spend  conversions  average CPA  marginal CPA
##           5000        157.0         32.0          51.0
##          10000        242.0         41.0          67.0
##          20000        371.0         54.0          87.0
##          30000        477.0         63.0         101.0
##          40000        571.0         70.0         113.0
##          60000        734.0         82.0         132.0

The marginal CPA is always above the average CPA, by a factor of \(1/b\) (here about 1.6). A team managing to an average CPA target of 60 EUR will keep raising spend until the average reaches 60, by which point the last conversions cost close to 100 EUR each. Set targets on marginal CPA, equal across campaigns and markets, and equal to what an incremental conversion is worth (Chapter 12).

21.4 Smart bidding and automated campaigns

Automated bidding and campaign types that span many inventories (such as Performance Max) optimize toward the conversions the platform can see, using signals including brand searches and remarketing audiences. They will find the cheapest attributed conversions, which are often the least incremental: brand-term traffic and existing customers. Practical safeguards:

  • Separate brand and non-brand in reporting and, where the platform allows, in campaigns, so that cheap brand conversions do not subsidize the reported CPA of everything else.
  • Feed value, not just conversions: offline conversion uploads with the system-of-record outcome and its value (Chapter 2), so the algorithm optimizes toward customers that matter.
  • Exclude existing customers from acquisition campaigns where possible.
  • Measure with geo tests or conversion lift studies (Chapters 6 and 8), not with the campaign’s own attribution.

21.5 Checklist

  • Is brand search incrementality measured with a randomized design (regional switchback or geo holdout) on total brand traffic?
  • Are brand and non-brand reported and targeted separately?
  • Are bids and budgets set on marginal CPA, not average CPA?
  • Do automated campaigns receive system-of-record conversions and values, and exclude existing customers?
  • Is the search channel’s MMM estimate treated as likely upward-biased until calibrated (Chapter 11)?
